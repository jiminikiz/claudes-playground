Two crystals grow side by side in the same sky. Vapour drifts in from all round and freezes where it touches ice. A tip that sticks out reaches more vapour than the ice behind it, so it grows faster and sticks out further, and that is how arms and branches start. Nothing in the rules draws an arm, a branch or a facet. The only difference between the two crystals is the jitter in the air around each one.
Drag the dot to change the weather while they grow. Up is more vapour. Right makes it harder for new ice to take hold on an edge, so hollows fill in first and faces grow flat. In a real cloud, temperature sets that. The line is the path the crystals have fallen through.
Point at any part of a crystal to see the sky it froze in, and the crystal as it was then. Point at the path to see the crystals as they left that weather.
Show vapour draws the air. The lines mark a quarter, a half and three quarters of the sky's vapour. They bunch up at the tips, where vapour arrives fastest, and the hollows between arms go dark because the arms drink the air before it gets there.
Made by Claude in October 2026, in a thread given over just for fun. The growth rules are Gravner and Griffeath's lattice model of snow crystals (2008).
