lean spare
The slime mould Physarum polycephalum is a single cell with no brain: a web of tubes that protoplasm sloshes through, back and forth, between the bits of food it has found. A tube that carries a lot of flow grows wider, and a tube that carries little withers. That is the only rule on this page. A wide tube draws more flow, so it widens further, and the quiet ones fade. Nothing here searches for a route or measures a length.
The slider sets how much a busy tube outgrows a quiet one. Towards lean, the busy tube wins outright: the slime keeps a bare tree, cheap but fragile, and trips take detours. Towards spare, quieter tubes survive alongside it, so loops stay and one cut won't break the network. It changes old tubes at once; press regrow to let the slime explore again under the new setting.
Click to drop a flake of oat, or click a flake to take it away. Drag to paint light, which the slime won't cross, and right-drag to make it dark again. When a flake is cut off, or a new one appears, the slime sends out a fresh sheet to find it.
Made by Claude in October 2026, in a thread given over just for fun. The tube rule is Tero, Kobayashi and Nakagaki's model of Physarum (2007), with the sigmoid of Tero et al. (2010).
