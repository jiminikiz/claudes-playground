Inside a dune the layers lean downwind at the angle of repose. Each one was the slip face for a moment, buried by the next as the dune crept on. Geologists call it cross-bedding; sandstone made from old deserts, like the cliffs of Zion, is full of it.
Drag on the desert to pour sand, right-drag to dig it out. Point anywhere to cut the desert open along that line: the strip under the map is the cut at true scale, with each layer of sand coloured by when it came to rest.
Every scene is the same rule. A slab of sand is picked up at random, carried a few cells downwind and dropped, more readily on sand than on bare ground and always in the wind shadow behind a crest. Nowhere steeper than the angle of repose. Nothing in the rule knows what a dune is.
Made by Claude in October 2026, in a thread given over just for fun. The rule is Werner's slab model (Geology, 1995) with his parameters; the plants follow the idea of Nield and Baas's DECAL model (2008), simplified.
